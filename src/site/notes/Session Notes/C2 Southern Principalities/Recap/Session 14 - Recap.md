# Southern Principalities — Session Record

## Session 14 Recap — The Job and New Logistics

Shōren Vale arrived at the Shigure Compound with a narrow document case, directions from the Quiet Fox, and a swan that had guided him to the hidden entrance. The master of petitions had come with an assignment, but first he wanted assurances that the crew could handle it. They wanted to know why the Quiet Fox's recommendation was not assurance enough. Producing proof of covert work was an awkward demand for people whose work was supposed to stay covert. The introductions became a prolonged argument over competence, trust, and how much a promise of success was really worth.

Time magic finally supplied the credentials. Shōren and a small group stepped ten minutes into the past and watched the earlier conversation from outside the compound. He could see himself speaking with the others, but was warned not to interfere with them—or with himself. They waited out the ten minutes and returned. This was proof Shōren could accept. His doubts settled enough for everyone to sit down over tea and hear the job.

The Quiet Fox had authorized an operation against **High Steward Aketsu Blackwing**. The crew was to assess the opportunity and, when they judged it viable, “remove him from the chessboard.” Bardune had made Aketsu the public face of its accusations over the Stillwater affair; In'nali still disputed them. Shōren warned that killing an official of his standing would have consequences beyond the death itself. He could explain Aketsu's habits and help identify an opening, but the reason behind the order belonged to the Quiet Fox. The crew received a target, not an explanation of the patron's motives or proof of the public allegations.

Research opened three possibilities. Aketsu lived with his wife and son in a guarded home in the Upper Citadel. He was also expected at an invitation-only diplomatic supper in the High Circles, where officials and representatives would discuss the Stillwater dispute. The third opportunity was a public address on the northern road. That last option was clarified as one scheduled address at one location, rather than a tour with repeated chances along the route. Its exposed approaches and limited escape routes presented problems of their own. The supper drew the strongest interest: the crew wanted an involved operation with room to mingle, maneuver, and play their parts, rather than reduce the job to the first available shot. The residence remained an alternative if reconnaissance offered a better opening.

The supper's guest list was easier to discuss than to join. They learned to expect roughly **40–60 invited guests**, **16–24 service and venue staff**, and **12–16 venue guards**, with officials bringing their own protection as well. Invitations would be checked, companions accounted for, and staff and deliveries admitted through a separate process. A disguise alone would not settle every access problem. The venue also had a mage responsible for its magical protection, but the crew did not yet know the specific defenses. Truesight and taste testers entered the discussion as possible obstacles, not confirmed features of the supper. Staff positions, forged credentials, distractions, and poisoning all remained approaches to investigate.

Before they could infiltrate a supper, they had to reach In'nali. The Great Lift was closed, its carriage stranded partway up and the gate at its base barred and guarded. The lower lift docks still operated, but clearance, manifests, and scrutiny made them no simple back door. The group planned to use an airship and skydive in ahead of the operation, leaving time on the ground to investigate and arrange access. Feather-fall precautions and an appropriately dramatic entrance received attention. Landing safely, however, would only begin the work; it would not provide papers, an invitation, or a way out.

The assassination discussion then produced its most memorable proposal: **turn Clay into an ant, get him inside Aketsu, and end the transformation**. Delivery, magical detection, and what happened to Clay afterward all needed answers, but the idea had enthusiasm behind it. Plan A acquired a literal meaning. Black fly and cockroach supplied alternatives further down the alphabet. Poisoning and an opportunity at the residence remained in consideration, while the crew debated how much of the ant plan could survive contact with the actual venue. The ant plan had not been attempted.

By the close, the missing documents and missing exit plan had become harder to ignore. Forging papers, taking someone else's documents, and seeking help from the Lotus Choir were proposed; none had yet supplied the crew with usable credentials. In'nali was already locked down, and removing its High Steward could make leaving much more difficult. The immediate agenda became the drop-in, paperwork, reconnaissance, venue access, and an escape route, with the operation itself still ahead. The crew had plenty of ideas for making an entrance. Leaving quietly required more work.

## Decisions and Consequences

- The time demonstration earned Shōren's trust and allowed the assignment briefing to proceed.
- Aketsu Blackwing was the Quiet Fox's assigned target. The reason for the order remained withheld.
- The diplomatic supper became the preferred opportunity, with the residence still a possible alternative.
- The crew planned an early airship insertion and skydive to allow preparation before the operation.
- The ant proposal remained an unexecuted plan; delivery, detection, and Clay's escape were unresolved.

## Current Situation

The party was still preparing for the operation. They had not entered In'nali, secured the necessary papers or venue access, or carried out an assassination. The city remained under lockdown, the Great Lift was closed, and the exact magical defenses at the supper were unknown. Both the final method and the route out needed further work.
