# Southern Principalities — Session Record

## Session 9 Recap — Find the Thread

The party's first evening together after four weeks of scattered work began with unfinished business from [[Mirror Lake Shrine\|Mirror Lake]]. They compared what each remembered of Sho's Timewalk, the stone beasts sent after the stolen shrine treasures, and the moment Nobu had died beneath the lake only to return through a history most of them never experienced. Sho admitted that he disliked using Timewalk because its consequences could reach much farther than the moment it repaired. The others made their own concern plain: if he was going to alter their history to save them, they wanted him to tell them what he had changed.

The argument never became a rejection of what Sho had done. Nobody preferred the history in which Nobu stayed dead. It did establish something the party had avoided saying aloud: Timewalk had become both one of their most powerful protections and a source of uncertainty inside the group. They had survived Mirror Lake, but they were still learning how to trust one another with memories only some of them possessed.

That conversation gave way to a new assignment from the [[Quiet Fox\|Quiet Fox]]. During Bakuryō's inversion, [[Compendium/Atlas/Material Plane/Rushthan/Droshar/Southern Principalities/Bardune/Bardune\|Bardune]] had begun constructing the **[[Compendium/Party/Quests/Stillwater Array\|Stillwater Array]]**, a contingency meant to contain a spirit too powerful to calm by ordinary means. Its three components remained apart: a spiritual Heart held beyond Bardune, four ritual Anchors hidden within the city, and an Accord Bell kept above it. Bardune intended to bring all three together for the Array's first complete assembly.

The party's task was reconnaissance, not sabotage. They were to research the components and their custodians, choose one thread, and follow its movement to the unknown convergence site. They were not to prevent delivery, and they were not to be caught. Whatever the Quiet Fox ultimately wanted from the completed Array would come only after they found where it was being assembled.

### Three Incomplete Packets

The Shigure archive still bore the marks of the flood. Updated riverbanks had been drawn over old roads, bridges and settlements were crossed out in red, and reports continued to accumulate around the maps. On another table waited three deliberately incomplete packets.

The **Heart** packet contained shipping records, sea charts, and agreements involving the [[Ebizo\|Ebizo]]. From those fragments, the party learned that the Heart was a fist-sized spiritual pearl kept by an Ebizo clan beneath the southern sea. The clan maintained an old trade relationship with Bardune, but its submerged communities, hidden routes, and controlled contact with the surface made it the most difficult lead simply to reach. Following the Heart would mean identifying the correct exchange and sacred escort rather than searching an ordinary shipment.

The **Anchors** packet held civic plans, conservatory records, and Hanimori correspondence beneath the clue: *four living stakes sleep beneath a garden that never closes.* Bardune's Hanimori conservatories were not warehouses. They were gardens, nurseries, clinics, and places of rejuvenation for living plant-folk whose health depended upon carefully maintained cycles of light, soil, water, and rest. Breaking into one carelessly would be closer to attacking a hospital than raiding a storehouse.

Maki worked through the correspondence for the intent hidden between its polite lines. The others divided civic plans, public records, natural histories, and direct observation among themselves. One set of plans betrayed a staircase descending beneath [[Compendium/Atlas/Material Plane/Rushthan/Droshar/Southern Principalities/Bardune/Rootglass Conservatory\|Rootglass Conservatory]] into a lower structure that did not appear anywhere else in the public record. That absence identified the conservatory connected to the Anchors and suggested that whatever slept beneath it was protected as carefully as its patients.

The records described four upright ritual Anchors maintained as sleeping, living structures rather than manufactured weapons. A Hanimori groundskeeper-engineer tended the sealed foundation below the conservatory. The party knew more about this lead than either of the others, but that knowledge came with a warning: an operation inside the conservatory would put them among people receiving care, and their usual talent for solving uncertainty with fire would be especially disastrous.

The **Bell** packet contained pilgrimage accounts, mountain surveys, and shrine histories. The Accord Bell rested at a solitary shrine upon one of the region's highest peaks, reached by an old pilgrimage path above Bardune. The surrounding mountains held many shrines and many lifetime keepers, however, and the party's research did not isolate the correct summit. The Bell was geographically exposed but still difficult to identify, and following its descent would leave few places to hide.

### Choosing the Anchors

The three threads offered different kinds of trouble. The Heart could vanish beneath the sea or pass through Bardune under illusion and sacred escort. The Bell had to descend from an exposed mountain shrine, but the party did not know which mountain, what the Bell looked like, or how large it was. The Anchors were hidden among Hanimori inside Bardune, but the party knew the relevant conservatory, knew about its missing lower floor, and could reach the city without first solving an ocean voyage or a mountain search.

After circling the risks, the group chose the Anchors.

Their working plan combined observation, disguise, magic, and distraction. They considered posing as visitors, watching the conservatory's traffic, identifying whoever held authority, using scrying once they had a suitable connection, and following movement rather than touching the Anchors themselves. Nobu offered his practiced card work as a distraction. Clay was prepared to disguise himself and remain close enough to intervene if subtlety failed. The group agreed that reaching Bardune and putting eyes on the conservatory came before committing to any intrusion.

Further investigation placed all three components on the same clock. The Heart and Anchors would move during the night **six days from now**; the Bell would descend the following dawn. The separate schedules used tides, watering cycles, and shrine observances, but together they revealed a coordinated assembly.

The party's reconnaissance around the conservatory finally turned research into something tangible. Their collection of disguises, distractions, observation, and intimidatingly suspicious behaviour unsettled a passing courier. Clay, playing the conspicuous drunken brute, frightened the courier badly enough that he fled and dropped a note. Clay recovered it before the opportunity disappeared.

The message revealed how the Anchors would move. **All four are held beneath Rootglass Conservatory, but they cannot safely travel together.** On the appointed night, the party would need to watch for four matching nursery carts leaving the same service yard at staggered intervals and taking separate streets—not a single guarded convoy. The dropped schedule confirmed the same six-day deadline and gave the party a physical object that may be useful for Maki's time-bending divination, **Ryoko's Revelation**, once Clay can be persuaded to hand it over.

### What Stillwater Was Built to Do

The party also uncovered the Array's intended function. Bardune began the project during Bakuryō's inversion, after appeasement and direct intervention had failed. When assembled, Stillwater should create a temporary boundary that prevents a spirit from escaping through water, earth, wind, mist, or spirit paths. The prison is not meant to destroy what it holds. Its purpose is to make negotiation or restoration possible when an inverted spirit cannot otherwise be reached.

That knowledge made the assignment more complicated. Stillwater was not obviously a weapon, nor was Bardune's reason for building it obviously malicious. It was an answer created by people who had watched a kaiju reverse their rivers and drown their valley. The party had been told only to locate the convergence site. They still did not know what the Quiet Fox would ask them to do once they found it, whether the Array would ever be activated, or who would decide which spirits deserved to be contained.

After several days of research and reconnaissance, everyone returned to the Shigure Compound with a time, a starting place, and one stolen opportunity. Night settled over the cedar rafters. The hot spring steamed. The Kappa waited beside six cups and far too many dice, loudly insisting it had never cheated before anyone had accused it.

The party had found its thread. In six days, they would have to hold onto it.

## Decisions and Consequences

- The party accepted the Quiet Fox's assignment to locate the Stillwater Array's convergence site without preventing delivery or being caught.
- They researched all three components: the Heart, the Anchors, and the Accord Bell.
- The party chose the **Anchors** as its reconnaissance thread.
- They identified [[Compendium/Atlas/Material Plane/Rushthan/Droshar/Southern Principalities/Bardune/Rootglass Conservatory\|Rootglass Conservatory]] as the Anchors' present location.
- They established that the components begin moving six days from the end of the session.
- Heart and Anchors move during the night; the Bell descends the following dawn.
- The party learned to watch for **four matching nursery carts travelling separately**, not one convoy.
- Clay recovered a courier's dropped schedule coordinating four separate carts from Rootglass.
- The party learned that Stillwater is intended to contain an inverted spirit long enough for negotiation or restoration.
- Sho's use of Timewalk remains accepted as lifesaving but now carries an expectation of greater honesty with the rest of the party.

## Current Situation

- The Stillwater Array consists of a spiritual Heart, four living Anchors, and an Accord Bell.
- Its convergence site remains unknown.
- Rootglass Conservatory holds all four Anchors in separated chambers beneath its public gardens.
- The Anchors will leave Rootglass in matching nursery carts at staggered intervals and take different routes through Bardune six days from now.
- The Heart is held by an Ebizo clan beneath the southern sea and will move under sacred protection.
- The Accord Bell rests at an unidentified high shrine above Bardune and will descend at dawn after the other components move.
- Clay possesses the courier's schedule; it may provide a connection for Maki's Ryoko's Revelation.
- The party has not seen an Anchor directly and does not yet know which nursery cart will offer the safest trail.
- The Quiet Fox has not revealed what comes after the convergence site is found.

## Memorable Table Moments

- Research into the Ebizo became an extended and increasingly hungry debate about crab people, lobster people, and the ethics of a seafood boil.
- The clue about “four living stakes” survived only seconds before becoming an investigation of living steaks and missed steaks.
- The party selected the hospital-like conservatory as its infiltration target and immediately had to remind itself that setting the plant people on fire was not a plan.
- Nobu found a practical field use for the card tricks he had spent downtime learning: distracting exactly enough people to let everyone else look suspicious somewhere nearby.
- Clay's proposed Hanimori disguise began and ended with the phrase, “I put plants on my body.”
- The planned hot-spring game nearly vanished beneath the research session, but the Kappa still arrived with six cups, too many dice, and a denial of cheating prepared in advance.

## Open Threads

- Where will Bardune assemble the Stillwater Array?
- Which of the four nursery carts can the party follow without losing the other routes—or being noticed?
- What can Maki's Ryoko's Revelation uncover from the courier's dropped schedule?
- Who is Rootglass's Hanimori groundskeeper-engineer, and how much do they know about the assembly?
- Who carries the Heart, and where will the Ebizo exchange reach the surface?
- Which mountain shrine holds the Accord Bell?
- Why does the Quiet Fox want the convergence site, and what will the party be ordered to do there?
- Can the party interfere with a defensive project built in response to Bakuryō without becoming responsible for the next disaster it might have prevented?
- How will Sho and the others handle future Timewalks when saving someone requires changing a history they will not remember?
