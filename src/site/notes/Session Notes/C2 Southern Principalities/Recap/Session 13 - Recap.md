# Southern Principalities — Session Record

## Session 13 Recap — Four Weeks of Fracture

The party returned from the warehouse with Nobu alive. The [[Compendium/Atlas/Material Plane/Rushthan/Droshar/Southern Principalities/Shigure Compound\|Shigure Compound]] gave them a quiet welcome, but the chamber beneath the warehouse was harder to leave behind. Around the gallery, they wondered whether its enormous restraints had been made for a spirit—or even a kaiju. That was speculation, not a discovery about what lay beneath In'nali. One of them went back through their notes and maps. The chamber was plainly purpose-built: its scored frame, chains, floor channels, wall conduits, reinforced doors, and locks belonged to one system. In the archives they found a water-damaged closure record, legible only enough to say the facility had been shut and its people would not return. It did not identify a present operator or connect the old machinery to the Array.

They gave themselves four weeks to recover and pursue their own business. An envoy of the [[Quiet Fox\|Quiet Fox]] arrived with payment: **4,500 gold pieces each** and a chest of magical goods. Sorting the chest took almost as much negotiation as the original job. Scrolls, potions, armor, a cloak, and stranger tools passed around the table; the proposed owners did not always remain the final ones. The party also settled something the last recap had left uncertain: Nobu had suffered close calls, including one undone by time magic, but he had **not** actually died. His companions' increasingly inventive ideas for teaching him caution remained jokes, not a punishment carried out.

### Work of Their Own

Clay went to the Asura compound. There, a master enchanter cut away older runes and set stronger ones into his weapon, bringing his ojutsu to **+2**. Later, uneasy that the green Oniborne might have seen and remembered him, Clay had the keepers rework his clay body and appearance. He spent his final week with the people at the compound, learning the altered body and helping with their work. The roads elsewhere were tightening, but the compound itself remained quiet.

Maki and Pico went looking for a shop in In'nali. Their first week of carousing produced a lead and three useful contacts, not instant ownership. In the second week Maki bought the business for **1,500 gold pieces** and put more money into making it unmistakably his. He wanted a confectionery with spectacle as well as sweets. Pico, sent to find an addictive ingredient, vanished for days. Maki sent runners and paid children to look for him. Jeff, a guard-outpost officer who recognized the boy, finally brought him back wrapped in a blanket, shaking and blue-lipped after a sting operation. Pico still held out a softly glowing amber stone: he had found what Maki asked for, at a frightening cost to himself. Talk of a rehabilitation business was a table joke; none was established.

Maki nevertheless continued the shop. He hired a salesperson, a baker, and a bookkeeper, learned that the glowing material came from stone mined below In'nali and refined with magic, and found two separate criminal sources for it. He arranged for the substance to be worked into the candy while its long-term effects remained unknown. Only his chef, Remy, was to know the ingredient; Remy would also be the public face of the sweets. At the week-four fight event, Maki's pop-up stall drew an older man who ate until he glowed, became ill, and collapsed. Another party member tried to turn the scene into a sale, then took the man's coin purse while moving him outside. The table did **not** establish whether the man died. The moment left a much sharper question than whether the candy would sell.

Other weeks went into equipment, contacts, and information. One member tracked down a koi-featured Ryujin enchanter in a mountain clearing and paid to strengthen the **Iron Kasa** rather than take its more elaborate variant. The enchanter invited them to stay while she worked; that week also became a lesson in making three spell scrolls. Sho worked with Jade, the enchanter whose forearm had petrified, to raise his Helping Hand Prosthetic from uncommon to rare. He also built up contacts and, with their help, got back into the In'nali archives. He found **no** document there identifying a spirit-binding facility. Property records did show odd, long-held plots of jungle outside the city, which he marked down without proving what they were for. Another lead concerned a guarded potion maker: a drop spot near Bardune could receive orders, while Daichi offered only the name **Yakuna**. Whether that name belonged to the maker remained uncertain, and a contact was asked to learn more.

The group's legitimate fight-pit venture ran another tournament in the fourth week, with paid staff, betting, and a candy stall. Its organizers treated it as an event business, not an illicit fight to hide. The recorded take for that event was **200 gold pieces**; the group discussed how Maeva would share in its returns and how the venue might be used between fights. Sho also asked Jade to accompany him to the event. No future romance or expansion was settled at the table.

### A Month Gets Louder

While the party tended to these lives, the consequences of the Stillwater job spread beyond them. In the first week, Bardunese guards questioned travellers at crossings and a broadsheet reported the dead at the trial, the missing custodians, and the missing Array. It named no culprit. In the second, the Array was publicly reported **recovered** beneath the abandoned In'nali warehouse after workers hacked through fresh roots. The same report exposed the forbidden spirit-binding facility. Bardune demanded an explanation; In'nali denied that the site was its current work and demanded answers about Bardune's secret weapon and the dead on the riverbank.

By the third week, both governments were arguing in public. Bardune pointed to the Array and the facility; In'nali questioned who had authorized the secret Array and why its trial had been hidden. Cargo checks lengthened, river crews were asked for papers, and some merchants took longer roads to avoid the crossings. In the fourth week Bardune named **High Steward Aketsu Blackwing** in an allegation of covert spirit-arming. In'nali rejected it. Neither side had made the first move toward war, but both were preparing for the possibility. These were public accusations, not proof that Aketsu ran the old facility or that the party had been exposed.

At month's end the party gathered again at the Shigure Compound. A paper swan settled on the steps and unfolded beneath a red wax seal. The message said **Shōren Vale**, a vetted associate of the Quiet Fox, would arrive **tomorrow** with their next assignment. He had not yet arrived, and the assignment had not yet been given.

## Decisions and Consequences

- The party received the Quiet Fox's payment and distributed the magical chest; Nobu remained alive.
- Clay upgraded his ojutsu and changed his appearance. The fate of the Oniborne he had knocked into the river last session remained unknown.
- Maki bought and staffed a candy shop. Pico returned alive but in grave condition after finding the glowing stone; Maki pursued two supply contacts despite the danger.
- Sho's archival search found anomalous land records, **not** proof of a live spirit-binding program. The Yakuna lead remained unverified.
- Bardune recovered the Array, exposed the old facility, and publicly accused Aketsu. In'nali denied current involvement. Border security and political pressure rose on both sides.
- The fight-pit event happened and made money; the sick customer's ultimate condition was not established.

## Current Situation

The four downtime weeks have ended, and the party is back at the Shigure Compound. The Stillwater Array is back in Bardunese custody. The party's role in its theft and placement has not been publicly named. In'nali and Bardune are trading accusations under heavier border security. Pico is alive but has been harmed by the glowing substance; the shop's product and its consequences are now an active concern. Shōren Vale is due **the following day** to deliver the Quiet Fox's next assignment.
