# The Shigure Kusarigama

Known in certain circles as **the Chain of Settled Debts**, this kusarigama has passed through three generations of the Shigure family. It now belongs to [[Compendium/Party/Player Characters/Player Facing/Nobu\|Nobu]].

## Appearance

The weapon is deceptively plain. Its sickle is forged from a dark, blue-gray metal unlike local steel, with faint patterns along the edge resembling water disturbed by rain. The metal never rusts, rarely needs sharpening, and catches the moonlight with a cold sheen.

Its chain links are square rather than round. Each was individually engraved with a single Dwarvish rune. Together, they form an old proverb:

> A debt ignored becomes a burden. A burden ignored becomes a disaster.

Near the base of the handle is the Shigure clan mark: **a mountain reflected in still water**.

Most mistake the mark and runes for decoration. Dwarven smiths recognize the work immediately.

## History

Nearly a century ago, a dwarven smith named **Durn Blackcurrent** left the western mountains and wandered the Southern Principalities. Unlike most dwarves of his tradition, Durn became fascinated by local craftsmanship and the ways mortals negotiated with spirits.

He forged only a handful of weapons during those travels. They were not made for soldiers or public champions, but for people whose work shaped history from the shadows.

One was commissioned by an early operative of the [[Compendium/Lore/Organizations/The Lotus Choir\|Lotus Choir]]: Nobu's grandfather.

The kusarigama later passed to [[Compendium/NPC's/Southern Principalities/Daichi Shigure\|Daichi]], who reportedly recited the proverb engraved upon its chain whenever he accepted a contract. It has now passed to Nobu, its third Shigure bearer.

## The Chain of Settled Debts

For nearly seventy years, every known bearer of the kusarigama served as a **Cleaner**. Its owners never became celebrated heroes, but their work left stories behind:

- A governor vanished after a failed coup.
- A smuggler lord disappeared without leaving a body or successor.
- A magistrate's books balanced themselves overnight after every witness suddenly recanted.

Old Lotus Choir operatives recognize the weapon. So do a few retired officials and certain crime families. None react comfortably when it is carried openly.

The appearance of the chain usually means someone important has made a mistake—and the matter has already been decided.

## Reforged Purpose

Nobu's reforging did not replace the weapon's history. It gave that history a sharper purpose.

Durn's strange metal readily accepted the rites worked into it, and the words upon its chain became more than a warning. Fiends are creatures of appetite without restraint: fury, corruption, and desire allowed to grow until they become catastrophe. Against them, the kusarigama carries each ignored burden to its final reckoning.

When the sickle bites fiendish flesh, the engraved links pull taut and their runes glimmer like moonlight across disturbed water. The weapon deals additional damage equal to Nobu's proficiency bonus against fiends.

It does not flare with holy radiance or reject fiends on principle. It simply recognizes what they are: **debts permitted to become disasters**.

## The Weight of the Heirloom

The weapon's importance has never rested entirely in its magic. It rests in expectation.

When Nobu carries it openly, older members of the Choir do not see him alone. They see his father, his grandfather, and every Cleaner who carried the chain before him.

Some approve. Some worry. Some are waiting to learn whether he is worthy of becoming the next name remembered in whispers.

# Hidden Data

## Durn's Metal

The kusarigama's blue-gray material is not ordinary steel. Durn may have forged it from dark metallic stone recovered near a mountain shrine after a meteor strike. Its exact origin remains unconfirmed and can be connected to a later location, culture, or threat when the campaign provides a natural answer.

The metal's unusual receptiveness explains why reforging can awaken new properties without erasing the weapon's identity.

## The Fiend-Bane Property

The reforging aligned the weapon's central proverb with fiends: beings whose unchecked passions and corruption turn burdens into catastrophes. This is especially resonant with [[Compendium/Lore/Deities/Ashura\|Ashura]] and the [[Compendium/NPC's/Southern Principalities/Aspect of Ashura\|Waiting Aspect]], but it does not establish that Durn created the kusarigama specifically to fight Ashura or that he knew Clay's history.

Keep the mechanical benefit straightforward: **when Nobu damages a fiend with the Shigure Kusarigama, the attack deals additional damage equal to his proficiency bonus.**

## Long-Term Direction

Each Shigure bearer may have left something in the weapon—not necessarily another mechanical enchantment, but a technique, reputation, unresolved debt, or fragment of history. Future growth should emerge through play and discovery rather than from a predetermined upgrade schedule.

The weapon's eventual significance is not merely that it became magical. Carrying it means Nobu has inherited a lineage of consequences, and his actions will decide what the Chain of Settled Debts means to its next bearer.
