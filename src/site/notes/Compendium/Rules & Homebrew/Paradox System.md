# Paradox System
## Malleable Time

In this interpretation, the past and future are mutable. Changing past events will result in a different present, often with unintended and unforeseeable consequences.

For example, you might misplace some valuable information about future lottery results during a trip to the past, only to return to the present and find that your long-time rival found that information and used it to become a millionaire.

## Temporal Meddling

### Temporal Meddling Table

| Paradox Points Gained | Temporal Meddling                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1**                 | Interacting with a past or future version of yourself.                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **1**                 | Acting upon significant information gathered from outside of your own time, or giving significant information to a creature that wouldn’t otherwise have access to it in its time and is likely to act upon it.                      |
| **1**                 | Extracting a significant object from the past or future.                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **2**                 | Killing or saving the life of a significant creature.                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **2**                 | Creating a bootstrap paradox (e.g. going back in time and teaching a song from the present to its own creator—who originally wrote that song?)                                                                                       |
| **3**                 | Influencing events in the past that are likely to result in sweeping present changes to an area or number of creatures the size of a city.                                                                                           |
| **3**                 | Retrieving a magic item from the past or future so that you now possess multiple copies of the same item, and again each day at dawn for each magic item you possess that’s the temporal copy of another already in your possession. |
| **4**                 | Killing one of your ancestors before they can produce the lineage that eventually results in you.                                                                                                                                    |
| **4**                 | Influencing events in the past that are likely to result in sweeping present changes to an area or number of creatures the size of an entire kingdom.                                                                                |
| **5**                 | Killing a past version of yourself.                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **5**                 | Influencing events in the past that are likely to result in sweeping present changes to the entire world.                                                                                                                            |

### Here We Go Again

In order to prevent a party from endlessly retrying a quest until they succeed, it’s important that there are consequences to repeatedly returning to the same point in time.

After all, they’ve already created a wound in the fabric of reality... now they’re rubbing chronosalt on it.

Aside from the added risk of accruing Paradox Points for running into other versions of themselves, extra Paradox Points should be given for actions taken to change a point in time that the party has already changed before.

As a rule of thumb, add **1 Paradox Point** to the values on the Temporal Meddling table for each prior time that the party visited that moment in time.

## Losing Paradox Points

Fortunately, wounds can heal—even abstract, metaphysical wounds in the fabric of reality. And just as well!

No one wants to travel back in time on an epic quest to stop a tyrannical wizard-king from rising to power, only to realise that their pub crawl to the restaurant at the end of the universe left them with so many Paradox Points that the slightest tweak to the timeline will now cause it to implode.

The party can lose Paradox Points in the following ways:

* The party loses **1 Paradox Point after a Time Break** (see *Time Breaks*). Once a Paradox Point is lost in this way, no further points can be lost in this way for **10 days** , although Time Breaks can still occur.

* The party loses Paradox Points each time they defeat a **Paradox Creature** (see *Paradox Creatures*) while in their own time. The number of points lost equals the creature’s **Proficiency Bonus**.

* The party can perform a **1-hour ritual to mend time**, which requires the participation of all party members.

In order to perform this ritual, the party must provide offerings in the form of gems or art objects worth a number of GP equal to or higher than:

  	  * **1,000 × the party’s Paradox Tier**

  	  * These offerings are consumed by the ritual.

  	  * In addition, each participating creature ages **1% of its natural lifespan**.

  At the end of the ritual, each creature makes an ability check using an ability and a skill or tool of its choice, as long as the GM deems it appropriate.

  For example, a creature may perform an **Intelligence (History)** check to recall the proper flow of events, or a **Dexterity (Weaver’s Tools)** check to literally sew a stitch in time.

  The DC for these checks is:

  **15 + 1 for each time that the ritual has been attempted**

  If half or more of the party succeeds on their checks, the party loses **1d4 Paradox Points** and the DC resets to **15**.

Other ways of losing points, such as specific items or abilities, may exist, as well as ways to prevent the gaining of points, like the Timekeeper Paladin’s **Paradox Purge** class feature.

### Thresholds

Sometimes, the spacetime continuum becomes so warped as it tries to cope with time travellers’ meddling that the knots formed are nigh impossible to untie.

Once a party acquires enough Paradox Points to put them in a higher Paradox Tier, it is extremely difficult to drop to a lower tier.

A party **can’t lose Paradox Points if it would cause them to drop into a lower tier.**

For example, a party with **15 Paradox Points** can lose a maximum of **1 Paradox Point**, as this is the minimum number of points for Tier 2 (**14–27**).

### Dropping Paradox Tiers

Dropping to a lower Paradox Tier requires a great deal of time and effort; it’s like untying a really complex and tightly wound knot.

The GM determines a goal that the party must achieve in order to do this, which should be on par with a **one- or two-shot adventure**.

This could include:

* Tracking and defeating an especially powerful Paradox Creature in its lair.
* Retrieving a long-lost Artifact brimming with ancient magic.
* Studying with a legendary sage that has transcended time and space.

If the party is still at the minimum Paradox Points for their tier once this goal is complete, they lose **1 Paradox Point** and drop one Paradox Tier.

## The Paradox Deck

### When to Draw

One card is drawn from the Paradox Deck for its **Temporal Mishap** effect in the following situations:

* The party returns to the present after any amount of time spent in the past or future during which they gained at least **1 Paradox Point**.
* A **Time Break** occurs (see *Time Break* below).

Unless otherwise specified, a Temporal Mishap effect applies to all player characters in the party, although the exact effects can vary between party members.

For example, a card that causes all party members to lose a treasured memory can cause the Wizard to forget the first book they ever read, and the Fighter to lose all recollection of their mother’s hugs.

After a card is drawn from the Paradox Deck for its Temporal Mishap effect, it’s removed from the deck and put away with the other cards of its tier that aren’t in the deck.

### Time Break

For every chrono-action, there’s an equal and opposite chrono-reaction... though when this occurs is unpredictable.

A party with **5 or more Paradox Points** rolls a **d20 each day at dawn**.

If the result is equal to or lower than their **Paradox Tier**, a **Time Break** occurs—the timeline spontaneously warps itself in an effort to become more consistent with the changes the party has made to it.

When this happens, the party draws a card from the Paradox Deck for its **Temporal Mishap** effect.

After a Time Break occurs, the party loses **1 Paradox Point** if another Time Break hasn’t occurred within the last **10 days** (see *Losing Paradox Points*, page XX).

## Paradox Creature

A Paradox Creature is a being that has been imbued with such quantities of chronomantic energy that time and space behave erratically in its presence.

These creatures produce powerful effects, determined by drawing from the Paradox Deck on **Initiative count 20** of each round of combat, making them more challenging to fight and keeping the party on its toes.

Any existing creature can be transformed into a Paradox Creature. To do so, simply add the following trait to its stat block:

#### Paradox Creature

**Paradox Creature.** On Initiative count 20 of each round, losing Initiative ties but before Lair Actions, draw one card from the Paradox Deck for its **Creature Warp** effect.

The effect takes place immediately.

Unless stated otherwise, these effects, including summoned creatures and spell effects, last until Initiative count 20 of the following round, ending when a new Creature Warp effect begins for the creature.
