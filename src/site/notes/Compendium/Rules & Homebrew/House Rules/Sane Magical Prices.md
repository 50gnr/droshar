Baseline price is determined by the contained spell level. Add the mundane armor or weapon cost where applicable. Enspelled Staff uses 90% of the listed baseline because expending its last charge can destroy the item's magic.

**Items That Won't Be Priced**

These items are intentionally kept outside the normal pricing system because their effects are too random, campaign-defining, powerful, or dependent on GM control to support a stable utility-based market value.

| Item | Why It Is Not Priced |
| :---- | :---- |
| Bag of Beans | Highly random effects, including campaign-scale outcomes. |
| Bag of Tricks | Creating permanent living creatures can have outsized economic effects. |
| Belt of Giant Strength | Can push ability scores beyond normal bounded-accuracy expectations. |
| Candle of Invocation | Historically difficult to price cleanly because of its unusually broad impact. |
| Cursed Items | Price individually, if they are sold at all. |
| Deck of Many Things | Too random and campaign-altering for a stable market price. |
| Efreeti Bottle | Potential access to wishes makes a stable price impractical. |
| Helm of Brilliance | Highly variable, charge-based power makes valuation inconsistent. |
| Iron Flask | Potentially captures or releases extremely powerful creatures. |
| Luck Blade | Potential access to wishes makes a stable price impractical. |
| Manual of Golems | Better treated as a blueprint; price the blueprint and crafting requirements for the campaign. |
| Manuals and Tomes (+2) | Often function more like permanent character advancement than ordinary market goods. |
| Potion of Giant Strength | Can temporarily push ability scores beyond normal bounded-accuracy expectations. |
| Ring of Djinni Summoning | Combines several campaign-changing capabilities in a single item. |
| Ring of Three Wishes | Wish access makes a stable market price impractical. |
| Rod of Resurrection | Often functions better as a plot-level resource than an ordinary market item. |
| Scroll of Titan Summoning | A hostile titan persists until defeated; this is a campaign event rather than sensible retail stock. |
| Staff of the Magi | Power level is too broad for a simple market price. |
| Tome of the Stilled Tongue | The source assumes an owner would be extremely unlikely to sell it. |
| Wand of Wonder | Effects are too random for a stable utility-based price. |
| Well of Many Worlds | Campaign-defining planar travel is better handled by GM availability than a fixed price. |
